About the magazine

A Magazine That Knows What It Is

Slop Illustrated writes about software made by one person and a rotating crew of machines. It is written with those machines too. That is exactly why it checks everything.

Why it’s called that

Most of what machines write is slop: confident, smooth, and wrong in ways that take a while to notice. We could have named the magazine something grander and hoped you wouldn’t notice. We’d rather say it on the cover and then spend every page proving we did better.

Who makes it

The editor is SLOPTIMUS PRIME, which is Kyle Durepos, who publishes asmojomast on GitHub. The projects are his and his agents’. This Living Edition was built in three passes, by Gemini Carbon, then Gemini Neon, then Claude Opus, each one asked to keep what came before and add something of its own.

The rules

  1. Every factual claim links to a repository at an exact commit. If we can’t link it, we don’t print it.
  2. We say what a project adds and what it stands on, and we name the people whose work it stands on.
  3. Pull quotes are pulled. Every one appears word for word in the article it comes from. We don’t invent quotes, people or numbers.
  4. Illustrations are labelled as illustrations. Screenshots say where they came from. Test fixtures say MOCK FIXTURE.
  5. When we get something wrong, it goes in Corrections on the front page, where you’ll see it.

How a new issue happens

A script, npm run issue:draft, reads recent public activity in Kyle’s repositories and drafts the next issue as plain Markdown. That draft arrives as a pull request. A person reads it, fixes it, and only then does it print. The machine can propose the issue. It cannot decide what is true.

What we keep about you

No trackers. Your saved projects, your reading ticks and your sound and paper settings live in your browser. If the server is up, your saved list is also kept in a small SQLite file under a random reader number, so it can show how many people saved the same thing. That’s it.
