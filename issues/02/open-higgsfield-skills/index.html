A Film Crew in Thirty-Seven FoldersA Film Crew in Thirty-Seven Folders

An agent-agnostic pack that teaches a coding agent to plan, shoot and cut a short film on your own ComfyUI, using only models whose weights you can download.

37 Agent Skills and a routing table of 70 routes. The 86-second demo ran 14 jobs on one Google Colab RTX PRO 6000 in about 8.5 compute units, including the roughly 99 GB model download.

  • Python (stdlib driver)
  • ComfyUI API Workflows
  • FLUX Klein + LTX-2.x
  • Qwen-Image-Edit + Wan
  • Chatterbox + BiRefNet
  • Google Colab / Local GPU

Hosted AI film studios sell you a prompt box with closed models behind it and a meter running. This pack does the same job in the open. Each of its 37 skills is a plain folder with a SKILL.md, stdlib-only Python and ComfyUI workflows, so Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode, Hermes or Pi can pick them up. Give video-director one line, say a lighthouse keeper finds a message in a bottle, and it writes a story bible and a shot list, picks a tier for your GPU (16, 24 or 40 GB), and keeps every prompt, seed and cost in one resumable manifest.

It has rules, and they are good ones. Stills come first: no video is rendered for a shot until its keyframe is approved. Caps are hard; when the GPU-minute ceiling is reached it logs cap_hit and stops, rather than rendering quietly into your bill. On Colab, spending waits for a human yes. And before that spend gate, it tells you that the LTX-2 weights use a community license that is not OSI-approved.

The honesty extends to itself. Most routes were checked statically and with dry runs, not run end to end on a GPU, and the README says so near the top. What did run is listed shot by shot: an astronaut on a red ridge, a fox in a yellow raincoat while the rain turns to snow, a vintage camera on a 45-degree arc, a lighthouse in a storm, a robot painting in a studio. At 61 stars when we checked, it was the most-starred repository on this month’s bench.

Under the hood

  • Zero-dependency orchestrator (`skills/video-director/scripts/direct.py`): stdlib-only Python CLI supporting `routes`, `validate`, `tier`, `run --dry-run`, `approve`, and `package --target google-colab`.
  • Stills-before-motion invariant: no video job is ever scheduled for a shot until its keyframe still reaches `approved`, saving expensive I2V GPU minutes on bad compositions.
  • Web-safe delivery encoding (`5a1a2d4`): `yuv420p`, H.264 High profile, and `+faststart` enforced across assembly and caption burn-in so MP4s play in browsers without transcoding.
  • Fault-isolated ComfyUI custom node loader (`motionfield_nodes/__init__.py`) skips broken sibling modules rather than crashing the whole pack.

What it actually does

  • Nine-stage resumable JSON manifest pipeline in skills/video-director/scripts/direct.py (intake, tier preflight, bible, shot plan, stills, video, continuity, audio, assembly)
  • Three hardware VRAM tiers: draft (16 GB), standard (24 GB), and max (40 GB) with hard retry and render ceilings
  • Data-driven 70-route decision table (skills/video-director/references/routing.md) extensible by adding a single Markdown row
  • End-to-end 86-second demo cut verified on a Google Colab RTX PRO 6000 (96 GB) across FLUX3 Klein, LTX-2.5 i2v, Qwen-Image-Edit 2511, BiRefNet, Wan Fun Camera, and Chatterbox

What it doesn’t do (yet)

  • The demo’s final cut, captions and title cards were made with ffmpeg, not the film-assembly skill. Still QA, shot QC, continuity and music were not exercised in that run.
  • No model weights live in the repo. You bring a ComfyUI server and download them yourself.
  • The workflows come from #OpenSourceHiggsfield hackathon projects. Their credits and licenses are kept in THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md and licenses/.

If you open the repo, start here

  • README.md: shot-by-shot provenance table for the 86-second Colab demo.
  • skills/video-director/SKILL.md: hard rules on stills-first gating, spend caps, and LTX-2 licensing.
  • skills/video-director/scripts/direct.py: VRAM tier presets (16/24/40 GB) and dry-run planner.
