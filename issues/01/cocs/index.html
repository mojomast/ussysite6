    Nine operators, seven harnesses, ten weapons, five vehicle chassis and up to sixteen bots in Combined Arms. One JavaScript simulation is shared by the client, the server and the tests.

    Online shooters run on a polite fiction. When you press fire, your screen shows the shot at once, while the real answer is still travelling back from the server. CoCS, the Colosseum of Competitive Slop, handles the fiction honestly. The server runs the match at a fixed 60 Hz and has the last word. Your browser runs a shadow copy of the same simulation so movement and aim feel instant. When the next snapshot arrives, it drops the inputs the server has confirmed and replays the rest.

    Snapshots go out 30 times a second as plain JSON over a WebSocket, quantized to the millimetre, with a delta codec and a full-snapshot fallback. Other players are drawn about 100 ms in the past so they glide instead of teleporting. There is no historical rewind buffer, and the reading notes say so rather than letting you assume one.

    You can play it now at arena.ussyco.de, or host a match on your own LAN with no accounts, no cloud and no inference service anywhere in the loop.

    Under the hood

    • Fixed-step 60 Hz authoritative simulation (server/game-server.mjs, tickDt = 1/60) with per-peer sequenced inputs and 30 Hz quantized snapshots.
    • Client prediction: a local shadow Match runs the player’s inputs immediately, then reconciles and replays pending inputs after each authoritative snapshot.
    • Snapshot-delta codec (protocol v2) over a plain-text JSON WebSocket with a full-snapshot fallback and millimetre quantization.
    • Remote actors, vehicles and projectiles are interpolated at an adaptive ~100 ms render delay; rooms support reconnect, a 20-second held seat, bot handoff, host migration and spectator seats.

    What it actually does

    • Live browser-playable 60 Hz multiplayer at arena.ussyco.de
    • Client prediction and reconciliation over a JSON WebSocket with entity interpolation
    • Nine operators, seven harnesses, ten weapons and five vehicle chassis
    • Self-hosted LAN game server with no accounts, cloud or inference service

    What it doesn’t do (yet)

    • Fixed 60 Hz authoritative tick (16.67 ms); snapshots broadcast at 30 Hz.
    • The client shadow predicts and reconciles; it does not rewind historical hitboxes.
    • Nine operators and seven harnesses; Combined Arms fields up to sixteen bots.

    If you open the repo, start here

    • Authoritative loop: server/game-server.mjs defaults tickDt to 1/60 and broadcasts snapshots at 30 Hz.
    • Wire format: plain JSON text over WebSocket: game/protocol.mjs wireSize measures UTF-8 JSON length.
    • Hit resolution: hitscan rays test 0.85 m × 0.85 m × 1.8 m axis-aligned actor boxes on the current tick (game/core.mjs actorHit).
    • No historical rewind buffer exists; the server reconciles predicted inputs to the latest snapshot instead.
